# Construction, parts and budget decisions

This inventory combines the completed-project reports, the hardware named in this repository, and the team's firsthand construction account. The two-person CS/engineering team selected and ordered parts, planned the cargo and battery layout, fabricated and fiber-laser welded the chassis, built the component tray and exterior panels, made and routed the power/CAN/PoE/network harnesses, and integrated the electronics and software. The engineering teammate led power calculations and wiring/fuse plans; the CS teammate led programming, model selection and integration with selected hardware. The final report's [build and system figures](build-and-system-figures.md) are included here, including the integrated power/network/CAN layout. This is a design record, **not** a priced bill of materials. No receipts, item quantities for every accessory, or total cost were available in the reviewed sources. Add them before publishing a numeric budget claim.

The team assembled a system from commodity parts acquired under a semester schedule and limited budget, rather than starting with a unified robot kit. The consequential decisions were how to supply and protect both drive and compute power, route fusing and conversion, build serviceable harnesses, choose CAN/UART/Ethernet/PoE links and make the resulting devices intelligible to ROS 2. The [report wiring layout](build-and-system-figures.md#power-network-and-can-layout) records the architecture; the exact purchase totals and some connector-level details remain to be sourced.

The [CAD and STL render gallery](cad-and-mount-iteration.md) shows on-the-fly mount and enclosure work for LiDAR, camera, VESC, PoE and CAN hardware. It is visual design evidence. The exact printed revision and measured installed offsets still need a fabrication log or matching build photos for each part.

## The steering decision

The fixed-wheel layout was practical for getting a moving platform within a semester. It proved drive torque, motor command routing and telemetry. The robot could turn, but tire scrub prevented the desired in-place rotation and undermined repeatable control for an autonomous route. This is a firsthand project outcome; the current bags do not contain surveyed turn paths or a controlled skid-steer benchmark. The next build should measure the baseline and compare two-wheel drive with casters, Ackermann steering and independently steered hub-motor pods before claiming better maneuverability.

## The LiDAR budget decision

The Unitree L2 made 3D point-cloud and LiDAR/IMU integration accessible on a student budget. Its value to this study was the hands-on work: understanding scan bands and message rates, aligning timestamps and TF frames, recording clouds, connecting Point-LIO and seeing where a motion filter would be needed. The team did not implement a validated Kalman-filtered state estimate. A roughly $420 purchase was a different design choice from a much more expensive outdoor-grade sensor, and the study makes no claim that this robot met such a sensor's field performance. [Unitree lists the L2](https://www.unitree.com/L2/) with target-reflectivity and environmental qualifications; outdoor range and robustness must be measured on OrinBot rather than inferred from those specifications.
